Questions studios ask us

How Playsignal reports work, which games they suit and what you get.

What is a Playsignal report?

A written playtest report built from public Twitch streams of your game. We follow each streamer's whole session and report the bugs they hit, where they got stuck, what they complained about and loved, how far each one got and why they stopped. Every finding links to the exact minute of the stream it comes from.

Where does the feedback come from?

From independent streamers who played your game live on Twitch. They say out loud what they think while they play, which makes their streams some of the most honest playtests a game gets. We only use public broadcasts.

How is it different from reading the comments or watching a few streams?

A single stream shows one player. We cover 10 to 25 streamers, start to finish, and count how many of them hit the same problem, so you can tell a one-off from something worth fixing. You get the minute of every moment, so your team watches only what matters.

Does it work for my game?

It works best when players go through the same content: narrative and puzzle games, horror, metroidvanias, platformers, action and adventure games. It's less useful for roguelikes, sandboxes, multiplayer games and visual novels, where every session is different or players mostly read. The free preview tells you whether there's enough streamed play for your game.

How many streamers do you need?

About 10 streamers with real play sessions for a Snapshot, and 15 to 25 for a Launch report. We check this before you pay anything.

Can you do it for a demo or Steam Next Fest?

Yes. A demo report arrives before launch, when you can still change the game. Demos are shorter, so the report is shorter too: where players leave the demo, what they don't understand in the first minutes, and what breaks.

When should I order?

In the first two weeks after your launch, a big update or a festival. Twitch deletes many recordings after 7 to 14 days, so the sooner we start, the more streams we can include.

Who checks the findings?

A person. Every finding is checked against the stream before it goes in the report: that it's really your game, that the streamer says what we quote, and that it's a real problem rather than the normal challenge of the genre.

What's in the free preview?

How many streamers and hours of real play there are for your game, the main finding in full with the minute it happened, and the headlines of everything else we found. There's no obligation to buy.

How long does a report take, and what does it cost?

Usually three working days. A Snapshot (up to 10 streamers) is $149, a Launch report (15 to 25 streamers) is $349, and a Publisher pack of three Launch reports is $890. If the report isn't useful to you, we refund you in full within 30 days.

Do I need to give you access to anything?

No. We only need the name of your game. Everything comes from public streams.

What about the streamers?

We only use public broadcasts, quote short phrases and always link to the original stream, so the streamer keeps their content and their audience. We never re-upload video or audio. Any streamer can ask us to leave their broadcasts out.
