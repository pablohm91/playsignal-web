Why streamers stop playing indie games

Short answer: almost never because of the game. We followed 225 streamers through 885 hours of 11 indie game launches. Only 14 sessions (6%) ended because of something the game did, and the most common reason among those was a bug. Most streams simply ran out of time.

Of those 14: 6 hit a bug or technical problem (a freeze, a crash, a soft lock), 5 were frustrated by a section, 2 got bored and 1 felt physically unwell from the camera.

A stream ending is not a verdict on your game. But the few streamers who quit because of it usually say why on camera, and the most common reason, a bug, is also the easiest one to fix.

Most bugs show up in only one stream

We logged 38 different bugs in the 9 most recent reports, and 8 of those 9 games had at least one. 29 of the 38 bugs (76%) were hit by a single streamer. The most widespread one left 7 of 26 streamers stuck in the same game.

Watching two or three streams will miss most of your bugs. You need breadth, and you need the exact minute: one sighting with footage is usually enough for your team to reproduce it.

Players struggle with explanations more than with difficulty

In 6 of the 9 most recent reports, the problem shared by the most streamers was something the game never explained: which button does a special move, where a key item goes, when a tool can be used, what an action actually does. Complaints about difficulty were rarer, and streamers disagreed about them more.

Look for the moment a streamer goes quiet and then asks their chat how to do something. That is usually a missing prompt or tutorial line, not a design problem.

Time spent not playing is a top complaint

In 6 of the 11 games, streamers complained about time spent waiting rather than playing: long loading screens, long walks back to a boss after dying, scenes or stretches they couldn't skip, and doors or sequences they had to repeat.

The audio mix comes up more than you'd expect

In 4 of the 11 games, streamers complained about volume: no volume settings at all, quiet narration or dialogue, or a few sounds much louder than everything else. Streamers balance the game's audio against their own voice, so they notice.

Separate sliders for music, effects and voices cost little and remove a whole category of complaints.

What streamers praise out loud: art and music

In 10 of the 11 games, the art or the music was among the things streamers praised most. Those moments, with the streamer's own words, are the best clips you have for your store page and your social posts.

Sessions are long

The median streamer played for 3 hours 10 minutes, and 82% played for more than 2 hours. 63 of the 225 reached the end of the game, most of them within days of launch. A streamer's session is a long, honest playtest that already happened.

How we got these numbers

  • 11 single-player indie games released in 2026: horror, metroidvania, action, puzzle and narrative adventure.
  • For each game, 12 to 26 independent English-speaking streamers, followed through their whole sessions rather than a few clips.
  • A person checked every finding against the stream. Streamers with sponsored content, the game's own channels and streams of other games under the same category were left out.
  • How a session ended comes from what the streamer said and did in its final minutes. We don't publish the names of the games or the streamers.
